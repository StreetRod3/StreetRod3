# Changelog

## 2026-10-29

The garage has a transmission scene, after the Street Rod 2 one. Clicking low on the car between the wheels (or pressing T) puts you under it on a lift, looking up at the back of the engine and the gearbox bolted to it by two bellhousing bolts. A click threads a bolt in, each click after that tightens it a turn, and clicking a tight bolt backs it out a turn at a time until it falls to the floor. With both bolts out a transmission jack rolls in, the crossmember comes out, and the gearbox slides back off the engine and down into spare parts. Select lists the spare gearboxes and raises the chosen one into the empty bellhousing with its bolts started but loose. The car will not drive out of the garage until both bolts are tight. The gearbox is drawn for its type (iron with a pan for automatics, aluminum with a rib per gear and a shifter for manuals, black and red for the racing box), and the ratchet, clunk and dropped-bolt sounds are generated in the script. Bolt turns are saved per car, and older saves load with them tight. Bought gearboxes now always go to spare parts, and the spare-parts folder's Fit button for a gearbox opens the new scene instead of swapping it in instantly.

## 2026-10-30

The garage has a creeper on the floor beside the car, after Street Rod 2's "Under the car". Clicking it (or pressing U) puts you under the car on jack stands, looking up at the whole underside with the front to the right and a slight tilt. The exhaust manifolds, the muffler, and the rear end are all in view at once. Clicking one (or pressing 1, 2, or 3) lists the spares for it, and fitting one unbolts the old part, lowers it, slides it out, and raises the new one into place with ratchet and clunk sounds. The old part goes to spare parts. The muffler can also come off for good, which leaves the pipe ends open and makes the engine louder in races. Every car starts with cast-iron manifolds, a stock muffler, and a 3.08 rear end. The newspaper has a new "Under the car" section with tube and long-tube headers, turbo and glasspack mufflers, and 3.55, 4.11 Positraction, and 4.56 rear ends. Headers and mufflers add power. A shorter rear end pulls harder in every gear but lowers top speed. Each part is drawn for its type, and the rear end carries its ratio on a tag. The old "Headers & exhaust" bolt-on is now the tube headers, and saves that had it fitted get it moved into the manifold slot. The parts list shows the three slots, and the car stats show the rear end.

The game has an icon: the chrome SR circle logo, kept full size in resources/SR3circlelogo.png and padded square and scaled to 512 pixels in assets/icon.png. It is the project icon, so it shows on the game window, in the taskbar, in Godot's project list, and as the web build's favicon.

The garage opens on a wide shot laid out like Street Rod 2's: the doors on the left, the back wall straight ahead with the clock in its middle, the calendar and the parts folder's shelf to its left and the radio shelf and the bench to its right, and the ceiling along the top. The room is smaller, with the back wall and the far end wall brought in, so the props are no longer lost in empty space, and the camera aims higher with a slightly wider lens. It stays inside the walls when you orbit. Every clickable is now on screen at the start. The row of tools nearest the camera runs, left to right, the creeper lying alongside the car, the wheel lift (now a low red floor jack with a long handle, which rolls under the side of the car to change the wheels), the spray gun, and the gas can.

Under the car, the exhaust and the muffler are now separate parts. The exhaust is the head pipes from the manifolds back to the muffler: a single exhaust, stock, crosses the left pipe over to a muffler on the right, and the new dual exhaust in the newspaper runs a pipe and a muffler down each side for more power. Each muffler brings its own tailpipe. Taking a muffler off leaves its pipe ending open where the muffler bolted on, and taking the exhaust off leaves the headers open, with the mufflers coming off along with it. The two mufflers of a dual exhaust can be different, and each gives half its gain; a second copy of any muffler can be bought so the two sides can match. Open pipes and open headers make the engine louder, and a dual exhaust's loudness is the average of its two sides. The single exhaust's muffler now hangs on the car's right side, and the head pipes run straight back from the manifolds to the muffler without doubling back.

Parts from the newspaper's parts list, bolt-ons and under-car parts alike, can now be bought as many times as you like, so each car can have its own set and a dual exhaust can run two of the same muffler. The ad shows how many you have and whether one is on the car in the bay. Fitting a part only takes it off another car when every copy you own is already fitted somewhere. Engines, transmissions, and wheels are still one of each.

## 2026-10-31

The game now has eight save slots instead of one save. Save and Load on the garage clock, and Load Game on the main menu, bring up the slot list, where each slot shows its car, cash, number of cars, and when it was saved, and the slot of the game in hand is marked. Saving over a slot that already has a save takes a second click. An existing save becomes slot 1.

The cylinder heads come off under the hood too, between the intake and the engine: they are one part, held by two bolts on each head (four on a V8), and the plug wires have to come off first since the plugs are in them. The engine needs its heads on and tight to run. Every car has cast-iron heads, and the newspaper sells ported cast-iron heads and aluminum heads. Saves from before keep the cast-iron heads, bolted tight.

Every bolt now takes two clicks each way, under the hood and on the bellhousing alike: a bolt threaded in from the tray runs down by hand and the next click tightens it, and a tight bolt loosens with one click and comes out with the next. A part just fitted still tightens in two. Each carburetor is held by two nuts instead of one.

Parts have a condition now, and spares can be sold. Everything bought from the paper is new, the parts a car comes with are 50 to 90 percent, and whatever is on the car wears one to three percent each race it finishes. A part taken off keeps its wear in spare parts. The garage's lists show each part's condition, and spares on no car have a Sell button: a buyer offers between half and nine tenths of the paper's price, likelier toward the top the better the part's condition, and you can accept or reject. Selling again after a rejection brings a different offer. The most worn copy is the one sold. Stock parts sell too: they are now copies like any other part, so one sold has to be bought back, and the newspaper sells them, at placeholder prices for now (the 283 and the automatic 3-speed as well). Only a set of wheels one of your cars came on does not sell. Saves from before give every car's parts a used condition.

Clicking the engine compartment (or pressing H) now opens an "Under the hood" scene after Street Rod 2's, in place of the tune panel. The engine is built up in layers: the engine on its mounts, the intake on the engine, the carburetors and the distributor in the intake, the air cleaner over the carburetors, and the plug wires from the distributor cap to the plugs. Every part is held down by bolts that turn like the bellhousing bolts: a click threads one in, each click after that tightens it, and clicking a tight bolt backs it out into a tray on the fender. With all of a part's bolts out and nothing on top of it, it lifts off into spare parts, so taking the engine out means taking the top end off first and pulling the plug wires. Parts lists the spares for each empty spot and lowers the chosen one in with its bolts started but loose. The engine will not start, and the car will not drive out, until everything is back on and tight and the plug wires are on; the hint and the garage doors' tooltip say what is missing. Spare engines and hood parts open this scene from the spare-parts folder.

The newspaper has an "Under the hood" section: an aluminum high-rise intake (the old performance intake), a tri-power intake for three two-barrels, a dual-quad intake for two four-barrels, a two-barrel carburetor, the 650 cfm four-barrel (the old four-barrel carburetor), a 750 cfm double-pumper, a dual-point distributor and transistor ignition that hold their timing better after races, and a chrome open-element air cleaner. Every car starts with a cast-iron four-barrel intake, a stock 450 cfm four-barrel, a points distributor, and a stock air cleaner. A multi-carb intake splits each carburetor's gain, and carburetors far too small or far too big for the engine cost power. Saves with the old carburetor or intake bolt-ons fitted get them moved onto the engine, and older saves load with everything bolted tight.

Tune opens a separate timing screen like Street Rod 2's: a close-up of the balancer and timing tab with a timing light in the foreground, flashing each time the number one plug fires, so the mark stands still at the timing. Advance and Retard turn the distributor two degrees at a time, and the tune-up sticker gives the timing the engine wants. Off the mark the idle drops and the mark wanders. The mark stays where the light catches it and has a red pointer on the tab to line up with. One step off moves it a clear notch, and the tab sits low on the balancer, clear of the belt, with the light held from the side. The idle is louder and the music quiet, and off the mark the idle lopes and misses, so you can tune by ear as in Street Rod 2. The hint no longer says how close you are.

The engines keep their own models, which the hood screen splits into the long block, the cast-iron intake, the carburetor, and the distributor, and a 350 V8 is in the newspaper. The engine mount bolts stand up through ears on the block's front corners, where they can be seen from above, and a bolt under the pointer takes the click before the part around it, so the distributor hold-down bolt no longer picks the distributor.

The engine sounds as Street Rod 2's does on an AdLib card. An OPL2 chip, ported to GDScript from ymfm (the emulator the Street Rod 2 port plays through), runs inside the game at the card's own 49,716 samples a second, and a port of Street Rod 2's AdLib driver plays the "motor" instrument from its sound bank on it. The note bends up with the revs and gets louder with them by Street Rod 2's own sums, laid over from its 1500 to 5700 rpm onto our idle to redline, and at the redline it jumps up as Street Rod 2's does against the limiter. The throttle and the gear shifts no longer change the sound, as they never did in Street Rod 2. On the tune screen a second motor voice plays against the first: the two bend apart with each step off the sweet spot and beat against each other, and play as one note when the timing is right. Off the sweet spot the idle still lopes and misses as it did before: the motor's volume is set a firing at a time, swaying in and out faster the further off the timing is, and a firing now and then misses and drops the volume until the next, more often the further off. Rendered side by side with the Street Rod 2 port's own driver and ymfm, the two agree on every sample. It all runs in plain GDScript, so the web build needs nothing new.

Under the hood, pulling the plug wires now lifts off any part they were holding down whose bolts are already out, the distributor first and then the intake, heads and engine in turn. Before, a part whose last bolt came out while the wires were still on stayed in the engine bay for good. A part already stuck that way, as in an older save, lifts off when clicked.

The spare parts folder now lists only parts that are on no car. Before, it counted anything not on the car in the garage, so the stock parts of your other cars showed up as spares, and fitting one took it off the other car. The Parts lists under the hood and under the car, the spare engines and the spare gearboxes go by the same rule, so a part has to be taken off a car before it can go on another. A spare copy of something the car already has is listed with its Sell button, and its Fit button is greyed out.

## 2026-11-01

The game can be exported for Windows, macOS, and Linux as well as the Web. The `Windows Desktop`, `Linux`, and `macOS` presets in `export_presets.cfg` write release builds into `build/`, which is gitignored: one executable each for Windows and Linux with the game data packed inside, and a zipped universal `Street Rod 3.app` for Intel and Apple Silicon Macs. The desktop presets include the same `.trk`, `.obj`, `.car`, `.register`, and `.json` files as the Web one, the Windows executable and the Mac app use the game icon, and the project now imports ETC2/ASTC textures, which the Mac build needs. The commands are in docs/setup.md. The Mac app is not notarized, so macOS asks before opening it the first time.

Clicking the car's name in the garage, or pressing C, opens the car list. A click or a number key only highlights a car: 1 through 9 pick those cars, and 0 picks the 10th. Switch it, the red button, rolls the highlighted car into the bay, and Enter does the same. Sell it is not wired yet. Forget it, F, or Esc closes the list. Switching to the car already in the bay closes the list first, then a speech bubble over the car says "Here it is!"

The spare parts folder no longer puts Sell and Fit on each part. A click or a number key only highlights one: 1 through 9 pick those parts, and 0 picks the 10th. Sell it, the red button, offers the highlighted part to a buyer, and Enter or S does the same. Forget it or Esc closes the folder.

In the garage, T changes the tires and L opens spare parts. Pressing the same key again closes that list. A opens the transmission. Inside the car list, L still sells the highlighted car.

The newspaper sells a 1950 Chevy Styleline for $900, a voxel model made in Goxel. It is built to the real car's size (5.02 m long, 1.46 m from the bottom of the body to the roof, 2.92 m wheelbase, about 1,430 kg) and comes with the 265 V8, the 3-speed manual, and the C1 steel wheels. The body takes the car's paint, while the glass, interior, and lights keep their own colors. The bumpers, grille, side trim, and window frames are satin chrome: half metal and very smooth, so they stay bright and catch the lamp, because full mirror chrome turns dark on the model's flat voxel faces. The model has no wheels of its own, so it uses the game's wheels, with the rear ones tucked behind the fender skirts. Car files can now give the body a `ground_clearance` in the model block: when a scene snaps the car to the floor, the body's lowest point stays that far above it instead of resting on the floor. The other cars leave it out and sit as before.

The car's name in the garage now sits in a white box at the top of the screen, as in Street Rod 2, and only the box opens the car list. The list fits its rows without a scrollbar up to eight cars.

Sell it now works, with Street Rod 2's haggling. You type a price into "I'll take $" and press Offer (O or Enter), or Never mind (N or Esc). The buyer has a figure in mind, 75 to 94 percent of what the car is worth, and a price at or under it sells. Over it, the buyer counters with "How about $…?" up to twice, and you can say OK or No and name another price; after the second counter the buyer takes anything within a tenth of it and otherwise says "No thanks!" More than the car is worth gets "No way!" and one more try before "Get lost!", and more than 115 percent gets "You must be kidding!" Under half its worth gets "You must be crazy. I'll take it!" A car is worth the paper's price, plus the price of each part put on it, less the price of each stock part no longer on it, and never less than a tenth of the paper's price. Wear does not change it.

What is on a sold car goes with it, and spare parts stay as they were. A car sold from the bay drives off through the doors and leaves the bay empty, even with other cars in the garage, and the list comes back so another can be rolled in. Selling the last car says "All cars gone!", and opening the list with no cars says "You've got no car, dummy!"

Every car now owns its own copy of what is on it, engines, gearboxes, and wheels as well as parts. Before, buying or winning a car with the same stock part as a spare used up the spare, and two cars with the same engine shared one copy, so pulling that engine off one of them lost it instead of putting it in spare parts. A spare keeps its wear when another car comes in, fitting a spare no longer takes the same part off another car, and a blown engine is always gone. Older saves get the missing copies when they load; a spare already lost does not come back.

Known bugs that are not fixed yet are now listed in `docs/bugs.md`.

## 2026-11-02

The fuel can in the garage is the jerry can model. It stands on the floor where the old can stood, and clicking it still drives out to the gas station.

Pressing Esc under the hood, on the transmission, under the car, or on the timing screen crashed the game. Esc now leaves those screens the same way Done does. While a parts list is open, Esc still closes the list and stays on the screen.

In the newspaper, A opens the parts ads as well as P. U and C still both open the used cars.

Buying a car from the newspaper brings it into the garage the same way a car comes back from the gas station or the drive-in: the doors are open, it rolls in from outside, and the doors close behind it. Folding the paper without buying still returns to the car already parked.

The garage clock menu now reads Save game, Old game, New game, then Settings, Quit, and Close. Old game is the old Load game entry and still opens the save slots. New game starts over the same way as New Game on the main menu, with the starting car and cash, and leaves the saved slots where they are. Save game is the red default button, the same way Sell it is in spare parts: it stays red when another entry is highlighted, and Enter opens the save slots from anywhere in that menu. On the Settings page, Enter still works on the control you are on. The main menu's Load Game button now says Old Game and still opens the save slots.

At the drive-in, when a rival pulls up, clicking the hood opens the same under-the-hood view as the garage, looking at that car's engine with its stock parts bolted tight. Nothing there can be unbolted. Close returns to the drive-in with the rival still waiting. Opening the hood a second time makes him say "Chicken!" in a comic speech bubble and drive off. Clicking the rest of the car still starts the race talk.

Taking an engine out used to leave the exhaust manifolds or headers stuck on the car, where they could not be seen, removed, sold, or moved to another car. They now go to spare parts when the engine comes out, and the racing camshaft, which had the same problem, goes with them. The same happens when an engine blows. A new engine needs a set of manifolds fitted from the creeper before it will run. Saves with a car that has no engine get those parts moved to spare parts on loading, and a car that has lost a carburetor no longer keeps its air cleaner.

Tires are parts of their own now, changed at a new tire machine against the garage's front wall. Every car comes on bias-ply tires, and the newspaper has a new Tires section with those, radials, and the stickier slicks. Tires stay on their wheels. To change them, take the wheels off the car from the wheel list, which puts them in spare parts with the tires still on and leaves the car unable to move. Then click the machine or press T, pick the wheels, and pick the tires: the old tires come off into spare parts and the new ones go on, and the wheels stay in spare parts until they go back on a car. The machine can also just take the tires off, which leaves wheels and tires as two spare items. Changing the wheels on a car puts the old set in spare parts with its tires on and brings whatever is on the new set, and wheels from the paper come bare. Tires on a set in spare parts are not spare parts themselves, and selling the set sells them with it. A car with no wheels can't be switched out of the bay, and a new car can't be bought until it has some, since it can't roll out. A car with no wheels, or on bare wheels, can't drive out. Grip now comes from the tires, not the wheels, and the jack's key for wheels is W. Wheels have a weight instead: the premium wheels are now magnesium and weigh 24 kg the set against 44 kg for the C1 steel wheels, which gives about 3 percent more pull, well under what better tires give. The standard steel wheels are a little heavier and pull a little less. Slicks already on a car in an older save move into the new slot, and a car with none gets bias-ply tires. The tires on a car do not look different yet, since the wheel models include their tires.

The camshaft can be changed now. It lies in the block under the heads, held by two bolts: with the heads off, back the bolts out to lift it, and use Parts to put another in. It cannot come out with the heads on, and the heads will not go on without one. Every car has a stock camshaft, which the engine needs in order to run, and the racing camshaft from the newspaper goes in the same place. A racing camshaft already on a car in an older save moves into the new spot, and other cars get a stock one. A camshaft still in the block comes out with the engine and goes to spare parts.

A rival's performance is now separate from the player's. Before, a rival's power, top speed, gearbox, chassis, and grip all came from the player's car, so buying better parts made the rival faster too. Now each rival runs on what their own car came with, in their own car's weight and handling, and on tires of their own. A rival who wins the player's car keeps it as it was that day, parts and all. Rivals in older saves start on bias-ply tires and what their car came with.

Rivals now come along gradually, each in a faster car than the last. The drive-in starts with one rival in a stock Fairlane, and the next turns up only after the newest has been beaten. The seven steps run up to a Ford Coupe with a 431 hp 350 V8, a 5-speed, and slicks, and a stock car can beat the first few but needs parts for the rest. They are set in `assets/opponents/opponents.json`. A car won at pink slips now comes with the rival's engine, gearbox, and tires, instead of as it left the factory. Rivals also pull harder in the arcade drive, where they were so far behind that even a stock car beat most of them.

The Corvette 1962 could not move in the non-arcade drive, for the player or a rival. Its wheel mounts sit too high for the suspension to find the road, so its tires never touched. The hub height is now brought down when a mount is that high.

Rivals can have a gender in `assets/opponents/opponents.json`, and the game's text uses the right pronouns for them: "She took your car!", "Her car: Corvette 1962". Anyone with none listed is they/them.

Worn tires now blow out. A set worn down to 15 percent lets go at a random point in the next race, which loses the race and any bet just as a blown engine does, pink slips included. The tires are gone rather than going to spare parts, and the wheels stay on the car, so it needs another set before it can leave the garage. The tire machine and the drive-in warn when a set is worn out.

The jack, the wheels, and the tire machine have sounds, all built in code: the jack pumping up and letting down, an air wrench on each wheel, wheels landing on the floor, the machine's motor, a tire's bead popping off, and air going into a new tire. A blowout goes off with a bang and the shreds flapping.

Changing wheels is much quicker. Each wheel used to make a long trip toward the doors and back, one at a time, for close to a minute in all. Now all four move together on a short route, and the jack is faster too.

The tire machine shows the change on one wheel instead of repeating it four times.

The wheels a car came on can be sold now, like anything else in spare parts, and once sold they stay sold.

Nothing comes off a car that is not in the bay. The code that could pull a part off a parked car to fit it to the one in the bay is gone, and the paper will sell another engine, gearbox, or set of wheels when the only one you own is on another car, where it used to say it was in spare parts and refuse.

Rivals have their own parts under the hood now, and a look under a rival's hood shows what they really race: their engine, its real power, and each part, camshaft included. The last two rivals' extra power used to come from no part at all. It now comes from a racing camshaft on the sixth, and a racing camshaft, a 650 cfm carburetor, and a dual-point distributor on the seventh, which leaves them with 400 and 431 hp and a few km/h less top speed. Win a rival's car on pink slips and you get it as they had it: stock if it was stock, with its parts if it had any. A rival who takes your car keeps everything on it, and that is what you will see under the hood. Win it back and it comes back as it left, with its headers, exhaust, rear end, wheels, tune, and wear, and a full tank.

Losing a car on pink slips now loses what is on it. The parts used to stay behind as spares as well as going with the car, so you could win it back and have two of everything. Spares you already had are still yours.

Blow your engine in a pink slips race and the rival gets the car with no engine, and can't drive it until they get a new one. They stay away from the drive-in for your next three races, and are listed as having no engine, then come back with the engine the car came with. You lose everything attached to the car, including what was bolted to the blown engine, and those parts go on their new engine. Win the car back and it comes with that engine and those parts.

## 2026-11-03

The Corvette 1956 now shows its real wheels. Its model had tires and hubs welded into the body, so whatever wheels the car had on, those were the ones drawn. They are cut out of `sr1-c56.glb` now and each arch has a dark wheel well, and the car uses the same wheel mounts as the Chevy, so changing wheels shows on it, and taking them off leaves empty arches. The wheels are a little smaller than the old fake ones, and the body sits about 5 cm lower to match. Its wheels are now where the model's arches are, a little farther apart than before, which changes its handling slightly in the non-arcade drive.

The Fairlane and the Ford Coupe now show their real wheels too, and for the same reason. Their tires are cut out of the models and their arches are dark, and the real wheel wells behind are kept, along with the Coupe's axles. The Fairlane's rear fenders hide the top of its wheels, like skirts. Both models now face forward by themselves, so the Coupe's car file no longer turns it around, and the Fairlane no longer sits 0.6 degrees off straight. The floating strip in front of the Corvette's bumper is gone. All three cars sit lower than before on the slightly smaller wheels, and their wheels moved to where the arches are, which changes their handling slightly in the non-arcade drive.

The three car models are now stored in Git LFS, as `.gitattributes` always said they should be, so cloning the repo needs `git-lfs`. The web build now fetches them.

There is a template for the Driver's Name screen, the 1970s California driver license, in assets/ui/driver_license.svg. It is drawn from a photo of a 1977 license at the same layout, 930 by 622, with the printed text outlined because Godot's SVG importer does not draw text. It imports as a blank card. A layer called fields holds sample values as live text, one id per value (field-name, field-address, field-birth and so on). They show in Inkscape, Godot doesn't draw them, and the screen will print the real values in the same spots. A stand-in portrait in that layer stays hidden, since Godot would draw it. The expiry year is part of the printed card, not a field: 1977, the photo's year, drawn by hand in indigo to look like its typed numerals. The screen itself isn't built yet. The seal is a made-up one and not the state's, and the fine print too small to read in the photo is a best guess.

New Game now opens the Driver's Name screen before the garage: a 1977 California license (assets/ui/DLTemplate.png) with the cursor on the name line. What you type comes out in capitals in the Special Elite typewriter font, up to 24 characters, and Continue or Enter takes you to the garage once there is a name. Back or Escape returns to the menu without touching the game in progress. The name is kept in saves, and saves from before this have no name. Sex, hair, eyes, height, and weight can be changed by clicking them on the card: left click or wheel up goes to the next value, right click or wheel down to the one before, and each list wraps around. Height runs from 4-10 to 6-08 and weight from 100 to 300 pounds in fives. These are kept in saves, and saves from before this get the card's defaults. The rest of the card (number, address, dates) shows fixed stand-in values for now. The blue portrait area is empty. As you type, the name also signs itself on the signature line in the Homemade Apple handwriting font, and long names shrink to fit. The signature is in upper and lower case and keeps any capitals you type inside a word, so McGee stays McGee while the typed line says MCGEE. A word typed all in capitals signs with just its first letter capital. Special Elite, Homemade Apple, and their Apache 2.0 licenses are in assets/fonts.
