# Changelog

## 2026-10-29

The garage has a transmission scene, after the Street Rod 2 one. Clicking low on the car between the wheels (or pressing T) puts you under it on a lift, looking up at the back of the engine and the gearbox bolted to it by two bellhousing bolts. A click threads a bolt in, each click after that tightens it a turn, and clicking a tight bolt backs it out a turn at a time until it falls to the floor. With both bolts out a transmission jack rolls in, the crossmember comes out, and the gearbox slides back off the engine and down into spare parts. Select lists the spare gearboxes and raises the chosen one into the empty bellhousing with its bolts started but loose. The car will not drive out of the garage until both bolts are tight. The gearbox is drawn for its type (iron with a pan for automatics, aluminum with a rib per gear and a shifter for manuals, black and red for the racing box), and the ratchet, clunk and dropped-bolt sounds are generated in the script. Bolt turns are saved per car, and older saves load with them tight. Bought gearboxes now always go to spare parts, and the spare-parts folder's Fit button for a gearbox opens the new scene instead of swapping it in instantly.

## 2026-10-30

The garage has a creeper on the floor beside the car, after Street Rod 2's "Under the car". Clicking it (or pressing U) puts you under the car on jack stands, looking up at the whole underside with the front to the right and a slight tilt. The exhaust manifolds, the muffler, and the rear end are all in view at once. Clicking one (or pressing 1, 2, or 3) lists the spares for it, and fitting one unbolts the old part, lowers it, slides it out, and raises the new one into place with ratchet and clunk sounds. The old part goes to spare parts. The muffler can also come off for good, which leaves the pipe ends open and makes the engine louder in races. Every car starts with cast-iron manifolds, a stock muffler, and a 3.08 rear end. The newspaper has a new "Under the car" section with tube and long-tube headers, turbo and glasspack mufflers, and 3.55, 4.11 Positraction, and 4.56 rear ends. Headers and mufflers add power. A shorter rear end pulls harder in every gear but lowers top speed. Each part is drawn for its type, and the rear end carries its ratio on a tag. The old "Headers & exhaust" bolt-on is now the tube headers, and saves that had it fitted get it moved into the manifold slot. The parts list shows the three slots, and the car stats show the rear end.

The game has an icon: the chrome SR circle logo, kept full size in resources/SR3circlelogo.png and padded square and scaled to 512 pixels in assets/icon.png. It is the project icon, so it shows on the game window, in the taskbar, in Godot's project list, and as the web build's favicon.

The garage opens on a wide shot laid out like Street Rod 2's: the doors on the left, the back wall straight ahead with the clock in its middle, the calendar and the parts folder's shelf to its left and the radio shelf and the bench to its right, and the ceiling along the top. The room is smaller, with the back wall and the far end wall brought in, so the props are no longer lost in empty space, and the camera aims higher with a slightly wider lens. It stays inside the walls when you orbit. Every clickable is now on screen at the start. The row of tools nearest the camera runs, left to right, the creeper lying alongside the car, the wheel lift (now a low red floor jack with a long handle, which rolls under the side of the car to change the wheels), the spray gun, and the gas can.

Under the car, the exhaust and the muffler are now separate parts. The exhaust is the head pipes from the manifolds back to the muffler: a single exhaust, stock, crosses the left pipe over to a muffler on the right, and the new dual exhaust in the newspaper runs a pipe and a muffler down each side for more power. Each muffler brings its own tailpipe. Taking a muffler off leaves its pipe ending open where the muffler bolted on, and taking the exhaust off leaves the headers open, with the mufflers coming off along with it. The two mufflers of a dual exhaust can be different, and each gives half its gain; a second copy of any muffler can be bought so the two sides can match. Open pipes and open headers make the engine louder, and a dual exhaust's loudness is the average of its two sides. The single exhaust's muffler now hangs on the car's right side, and the head pipes run straight back from the manifolds to the muffler without doubling back.

Parts from the newspaper's parts list, bolt-ons and under-car parts alike, can now be bought as many times as you like, so each car can have its own set and a dual exhaust can run two of the same muffler. The ad shows how many you have and whether one is on the car in the bay. Fitting a part only takes it off another car when every copy you own is already fitted somewhere. Engines, transmissions, and wheels are still one of each.

## 2026-10-31

Clicking the engine compartment (or pressing H) now opens an "Under the hood" scene after Street Rod 2's, in place of the tune panel. The engine is built up in layers: the engine on its mounts, the intake on the engine, the carburetors and the distributor in the intake, the air cleaner over the carburetors, and the plug wires from the distributor cap to the plugs. Every part is held down by bolts that turn like the bellhousing bolts: a click threads one in, each click after that tightens it, and clicking a tight bolt backs it out into a tray on the fender. With all of a part's bolts out and nothing on top of it, it lifts off into spare parts, so taking the engine out means taking the top end off first and pulling the plug wires. Parts lists the spares for each empty spot and lowers the chosen one in with its bolts started but loose. The engine will not start, and the car will not drive out, until everything is back on and tight and the plug wires are on; the hint and the garage doors' tooltip say what is missing. Spare engines and hood parts open this scene from the spare-parts folder.

The newspaper has an "Under the hood" section: an aluminum high-rise intake (the old performance intake), a tri-power intake for three two-barrels, a dual-quad intake for two four-barrels, a two-barrel carburetor, the 650 cfm four-barrel (the old four-barrel carburetor), a 750 cfm double-pumper, a dual-point distributor and transistor ignition that hold their timing better after races, and a chrome open-element air cleaner. Every car starts with a cast-iron four-barrel intake, a stock 450 cfm four-barrel, a points distributor, and a stock air cleaner. A multi-carb intake splits each carburetor's gain, and carburetors far too small or far too big for the engine cost power. Saves with the old carburetor or intake bolt-ons fitted get them moved onto the engine, and older saves load with everything bolted tight.

Tune opens a separate timing screen like Street Rod 2's: a close-up of the balancer and timing tab with a timing light in the foreground, flashing each time the number one plug fires, so the mark stands still at the timing. Advance and Retard turn the distributor two degrees at a time, and the tune-up sticker gives the timing the engine wants. Off the mark the idle drops and the mark wanders. The mark stays where the light catches it and has a red pointer on the tab to line up with. One step off moves it a clear notch, and the tab sits low on the balancer, clear of the belt, with the light held from the side. The idle is louder and the music quiet, and off the mark the idle lopes and misses, so you can tune by ear as in Street Rod 2. The hint no longer says how close you are.

The engines keep their own models, which the hood screen splits into the long block, the cast-iron intake, the carburetor, and the distributor, and a 350 V8 is in the newspaper. The engine mount bolts stand up through ears on the block's front corners, where they can be seen from above, and a bolt under the pointer takes the click before the part around it, so the distributor hold-down bolt no longer picks the distributor.
